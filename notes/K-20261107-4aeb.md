Symptom: a mouse (or butterfly) passing very close to a corner glitches, turns almost 360 degrees, and can then walk off in another direction, even back the way it came.
Root cause (in `simpleGeoCreatureBehavior.ts`): the geodesic passes the vertex at a few cm, inside body radius + zone radius, so the side rule sends the body round the zone on a ~0.8 m radius. The leash was the straight-line creature-leader distance (0.5 m), so it froze the leader behind the zone while the creature was merely off to the side. The creature then got ahead of the frozen leader around the vertex; every later step back toward it pointed into the zone, and `applyForbiddenZoneSideRule` replaced it by a full-length tangent in the required direction, so the creature went round the whole cone.
Lasting damage: that extra loop round the vertex composes the vertex's holonomy into `creatureFromLeader` (rotation = angle deficit: cube 90, dodecahedron 36, icosahedron 60, octahedron 120, tetrahedron 180 deg; measured exactly these). The leader stays on its geodesic, but the creature steers at the leader's image rotated about the vertex, i.e. walks a different geodesic (cube front-runner: 10.8 m off its own, for 46 s until another loop undid it). Nothing self-corrects a wrong map.
Trap for probes: comparing the creature with `composeRigidTransform3(creatureFromLeader, leader.localPose)` cannot see this, because it measures through the corrupted map. Check instead (a) that the map's rotation is identity whenever creature and leader share a cell (not valid in single-cell worlds like genus-2, where it blips for one frame at portal crossings), or (b) the distance from the creature to the unobstructed geodesic walked with `walkDevelopedSegment` from the start pose (restrict to segments in the creature's cell; a geodesic running along an edge shows false spikes when the wobble crosses the edge, and on a torus a long walk fills the cell).
Failed approaches: (1) stopping when tangent . desired < 0 deadlocks (leader stays frozen behind; head-on the dot is ~0 and noisy, so the creature waits for ever while the leader walks away). (2) An along-track leash alone still leaves big turns (the 0.35 m forward collision offset lets the body get ahead around the zone).
Fix: the leash measures the lead along the leader's forward axis only; `isTargetBehindAroundZone` (target less than a quarter turn behind on the required way round the zone centre, from the creature origin) makes the creature stop at the zone and sets `waitingForLeader`, which lets the leader advance whatever the leash says. A geodesic passing the vertex on that side always moves round it in that direction, so the leader cannot run away.
Evidence: 1800 s over every example creature: no turn > 216 deg in 5 s, map identity in shared cells, creature within ~0.7 m of its own geodesic apart from edge artefacts, longest pause 0.37 s. Guards in `tests/world-objects/simpleGeoCreatureStall.test.ts`: 'does not circle a vertex...' and 'stays on its own geodesic after passing a vertex closely'.
Remaining exposure (not observed): the map is right only while the creature passes every vertex on the leader's side, and the side rule acts only on contact with a zone disc; a large outside push (player body, depenetration, wrong-side orbit) that carries the creature round a vertex outside the disc would corrupt it the same way.
