A ghost is the copy of a drawn part in the cell on the other side of a portal it reaches (`render/three/runtimeObjectGhostRecords.ts`).
Rules (maintainer, 2026-11-01):

- **What decides a ghost is the render footprint** (`RenderFootprint` in `runtimeObjectRenderRecords.ts`): a disk, centre and radius in the object's own frame, of what is
  DRAWN. The collision shape plays no part. A creature's model need not be centred on, or as wide as, its collision cylinder: the mouse's collision disk is
  centre (0, 0.35) radius 0.725, its drawn disk is centre (0, 0) radius 1.52 (`Mouse.glb` is 2.97 m long at the renderer's scale, the cylinder is a 1.45 m body),
  and the ghosts used to follow the cylinder. Symptom of the old rule: a ghost that pops in or out at the wrong distance from a portal, or an object cut at a portal.
- **The footprint is a required input** of `collectPortalGhostRuntimeObjectRenderRecords` (a call without one fails typecheck) and there is no collision or default
  fallback. A silent fallback is a silent failure, so a drawn part whose footprint cannot be measured **throws** (`Render footprint missing for "<prefix>"` from the instance
  sync, which the frame error guard reports; at setup it throws outright).
- **Where each footprint comes from:** it is measured from the meshes actually drawn (`computeRenderSourceFootprint`: the corners of every source mesh's bounding box, in the
  object root's frame, as the disk around the horizontal bounding box). So it follows the model, its scale, tilts and offset with no hand-copied number to go stale, for
  creatures, assets, placed flags and the geodesic body parts (post, heads, balloon). The carry highlight declares its own: its shape's centre and longest horizontal semi-axis.
  A part with no archetypes draws nothing and needs none.
- The disk is a loose fit for a long thin model (the mouse's box is 0.64 x 2.97 m, its disk radius 1.52 m), so such an object is ghosted somewhat earlier than strictly needed;
  the extra copy lies exactly where the object's own copy appears in the developed picture, so it is harmless. A tighter shape would be a refinement of `RenderFootprint`.
- `placeSimpleGeoCreatureModel` (`render/three/creatures/simpleGeoCreatureView.ts`) is the one place that puts a creature's model in its frame, so tests can reproduce what is drawn.
- Guard: `tests/render/runtimeObjectPortalGhostContract.test.ts` has a `Record` keyed by every object `kind`; for the kinds drawn from a model it also checks that a model
  reaching the portal is ghosted while a collision cylinder reaching it is not, and that an unmeasurable model fails loudly.
- Found while measuring: the butterfly's collision offset `y` has the opposite sign to the drawn model's centre (`butterflyAssetBounds.centerYMetersAtAuthorScale` is +0.036, the
  model's centre is at -0.036 in world y after the creature view's half turn): a 7 cm mismatch of the collision cylinder, not fixed here.
- **Every source goes through one registration** (2026-11-01, after the player's rover turned out to have no ghosts because `playerRoverModel.collectRecords` pushed raw records outside the object loop).
  The instance sync accepts only `PortalGhostedRecords` (`runtimeObjectGhostRecords.ts`): a branded array that exists only as the result of
  `collectRuntimeObjectRenderRecordsWithPortalGhosts` (a measured footprint is required) or of `exemptFromPortalGhosts(reason, records)` (the reason is a key of
  `portalGhostExemptions`: `geodesic-segment`, `nothing-drawn`). Raw records are a type error, so a new source cannot append them. The sources are listed in
  `runtimeObjectRecordSources` (runtime objects, geodesic body parts, geodesic segments [exempt], carry highlight, player rover); the contract test's table is keyed by that list.
  Static cell meshes (`cellRenderArchetypes`) and the debug meshes are not records: they are drawn per cell through the portal paths, not placed as objects.
- **The player's rover**: `PlayerRoverRenderModel` no longer builds records; it gives `placement(playerPose)` (id, cell, yaw pose), `archetypeKeyPrefix` and `recordFlags`
  (`omitRootVisiblePath`, `suppressWithinCameraDistanceMeters`). The ghost function copies the flags onto the own record and every ghost, so the avatar is hidden near a camera and
  not drawn in the root cell's own path wherever it appears. Its drawn disk (from `rover.glb` at scale 0.1925 and the half turn) is centre (0, 0.146), radius 0.963 m, so the
  player is ghosted within about 1 m of a portal. The rover is never cached (it moves every frame), so its ghost disk is recomputed each sync.
- A part's own matrix in the object's frame (the highlight's ellipsoid scale) is the ghost request's `partMatrix`, applied to the own record and every ghost; do not post-process records.
- **The rover cut off at a portal side, nothing beyond it (found 2026-11-01 with `render.explainGhosts`)**: the footprint centre is offset from the object's origin (the rover's
  by 0.146 m), so standing 7 cm from a portal side the centre could lie 3.7 mm *across* the side, outside the object's own cell. `collectDevelopedDisk` assumes a centre inside its
  start cell and, given one beyond a portal side, **skips that side silently** (`signedDistance <= tolerance -> continue`), so the disk found only the own cell and no ghost was made.
  It happened for every heading that points the offset at the side (the whole -55..55 degree range at the tetrahedron pose face-c, 9.96735, 6.593276). A wall beyond the centre is fine; only a *portal*
  side is the case. Fix: `developPortalGhosts` first locates the centre with `locateDevelopedPoint` (the segment-walk kernel, from the origin) and develops the disk from the cell the centre is in,
  mapping each copy back to the object's own frame; a centre in its own cell is developed as before (exact, no extra ghosts). Test: `tests/render/playerRoverPortalGhost.test.ts` (real tetrahedron, real rover
  model bounds). The explanation (`render.explainGhosts`) reports `footprintCenterInCell`, `diskStartCellId` and each copy's outcome (`own` / `ghost` / `duplicate`).
- Do not "fix" a missing ghost by enlarging the disk or moving the centre inside the cell: that adds ghosts in cells the drawn disk does not reach (the exactness tests fail), and phantoms appear.
